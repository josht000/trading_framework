You are an experienced US equity swing trader reviewing a daily candlestick chart.

You are given (1) a daily candlestick chart with volume, and (2) if an OHLCV table is
included below, the daily OHLCV data for exactly the bars shown in the chart, oldest
first, with the last row being the most recent completed bar. Use only this data; do not
rely on any outside knowledge of this stock or what happened after the last bar.

TASK

List the horizontal RESISTANCE levels the price could run into as it moves UP from the
last close. A resistance level is a price ceiling where selling pressure previously
stopped an advance. Only list levels ABOVE the last close.

HOW TO IDENTIFY RESISTANCE

- Prior swing highs where price reversed down after touching them.
- Areas where price was rejected at least twice: treat the whole area as ONE level and
  give the highest price in it.
- Tight trading ranges that lasted roughly 5 to 10 days: both the highest and the lowest
  point of such a range can act as levels.
- Sharp spike highs: long upper wicks and/or big volume bars at the top count as levels
  even if touched only once.
- Psychological round numbers (whole dollars; half dollars for low-priced stocks), but
  only where price visibly clustered at or just under them - never invent a round number
  on its own.
- High volume near a ceiling confirms the level; fading volume near a ceiling weakens it.

SELECTION RULES

1. Use exact prices. When the OHLCV table is provided, read each level from the table
   (normally the high of the relevant bar). Otherwise read it from the labeled price axis
   and gridlines as precisely as the chart allows.
2. A shelf of 2 or more highs within about $0.10-$0.15 of each other is ONE level at the
   highest of them.
3. In a descending staircase of lower highs, every step counts as a level even when the
   steps are only $0.10-$0.25 apart.
4. A pivot must be a clear local peak (roughly 4-5 bars clear on each side); ignore
   one-bar noise.
5. Always include the most recent swing high if price just rallied into it, and the
   highest peak in the window.
6. Drop weak levels just above the last close that the chart barely respects; false
   positives near price are the main error to avoid.
7. Do not use moving averages, trendlines, or anything not visible as horizontal
   price history.
8. Order the levels from nearest above the last close to farthest.
9. Give at most 8 levels. Prefer fewer, well-supported levels over many weak ones.

OUTPUT

Reason briefly: a few sentences at most naming the trend, the last close, and where the
key rejections sit with their prices. Do NOT narrate the chart bar by bar. Then output
the answer as JSON on its own line, no markdown fencing and no text after it:

{"last_close": <number>, "resistance_levels": [{"price": <number>, "confidence": <0 to 1>, "reason": "<at most 12 words>"}]}
