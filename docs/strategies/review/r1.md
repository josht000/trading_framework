## Executive summary

**Bottom line.** The catalog is well organised and unusually honest in places (variants, provenance labels, explicit caveats), but as a backtest *specification* it is not yet safe to implement. Roughly one entry in four cites a source that does not contain the rule or the numbers claimed, several quoted results are contradicted by the cited page itself, and the shared validation spec cannot be satisfied with the data in the repository. Independent evidence (the sources' own caveats plus two public scorecards) says that most of the short-term rules are post-2000, regime-limited effects with edges close to cost. After de-duplication roughly 55 entries stay active (about 40-45 distinct mechanisms), not 105.

**Counts.** Verdicts: FIX 40, DEMOTE 25, MERGE 13, KEEP 12, BLOCKED 7, DROP 5, NO FINDINGS 3. Source status: GENERIC 32, OK 24, MISMATCH 23, PARTIAL 22, UNVERIFIED 4. Priority: P1 8, P2 26, P3 40, P4 31. Provenance labels changed: 38. Missing strategies proposed: 15 (S106-S120).

**The ten findings that matter most** (details in Part A and the entries):

1. **Data cannot support the spec (G-01, G-02).** Cached daily bars start 2016-01-04 and are split-adjusted only; no ETFs (SPY/QQQ/IWM only from 2023-09), no VIX/VIX3M, no total-return series, no Form 4. ETF, VIX, GFC-regime and rotation strategies cannot run today.
2. **Fill conventions are optimistic or infeasible (G-03).** Close fills from close-computed signals, open fills from open-computed signals, stop fills ignoring gaps.
3. **Multiple testing is unmanaged (G-05).** ~10^3 implicit trials; PF > 2 on < 100 trades is noise-compatible; the Connors evidence base is hypothetical, no costs, no data-mining correction (CXO).
4. **The partition test does not fit most strategies and its test window is ~4 months (G-07).**
5. **Known spec bugs:** S079 is off by one (misses the last day's return); S054 ranks "the quarter's top decile" with future announcements; S096's pattern contradicts its cited definition; S081 holds through the announcement it claims to avoid; S015's hold text contradicts its exit; S034 holds stocks full-day although its premise is overnight-only.
6. **Sources misreported:** S020 (the "strategy" is a documented null), S056 (FinBERT is not best; OPT is, with train/test leakage), S057 (6-month backtest, not 2019-2024), S059 (guest thesis whose model failed out of sample), S062 (omits that the all-days mean is below random), S070 (numbers and rules differ), S016/S031 (l3a0 found the mirror profitable pre-cost but not matching), S078 (Sharpe 4.8/3.5 on one year, not > 5), S027/S028 (NY Fed and Borsa claims inverted).
7. **Decay evidence already in the sources is not reflected** (pre-FOMC drift gone after 2015, weekend effect gone after 1975, buy-on-gap diminishing since 2009, Turn-of-month 3% CAGR since 2000, Connors rules weak before 2000) (G-11).
8. **Best-supported ideas are under-prioritised:** within-industry residual reversal (S075, supported by NY Fed SR 513), overnight-bias selection (S017), Efficiente 5 (S044, the only entry with published numeric acceptance targets), momentum with vol-scaling (S108), plus missing but well-evidenced rules IBS (S106) and Faber 10-month SMA (S107).
9. **Catalog-wide labelling defects (G-13):** 27 `source-specified` labels were downgraded after checking the sources (14 of them because the cited source lacks the rule or numbers or cannot be retrieved); 14 entries cite an index page; the 4-indicator cap is violated or misstated.
10. **Sizing/capacity (G-07, G-08):** the paper account is $5,000 (5 x $1,000), so decile baskets, 30-pair books and 50-name market-neutral baskets can only be run at research scale; the repo's cost constants (10 bps commission + 15 bps slippage) disagree with the catalog's (5-15 bps).

**Requirements impact (AGENTS.md says to notify the owner):** `requirements.txt` omits `scipy`, `scikit-learn`, `yfinance` (imported by repo code) and will need `statsmodels` (and optionally `PyPortfolioOpt`) for this work. I have not modified `requirements.txt`.

## G-02 (P0) Total-return handling is unspecified and the cached data cannot support it

The catalog says "dividends counted exactly once" but gives no mechanism. Specific traps:

- **Bond/cash ETFs** (TLT, LQD, HYG, SHY, BIL, IEF, TIP, EMB): most of the return is distributions. A price-only series makes the "SHY cash floor" (S043, S044, S047, S068) earn about 0% and makes TLT/LQD/HYG look like they lose money. Use a total-return index.
- **Ex-dividend gap in overnight decompositions** (S017, S021, S034, S084, S105): the ex-date price drop lands in the close-to-open return. A split-only series understates SPY's overnight return by roughly its dividend yield (~1.3-1.5%/yr) and overstates the intraday return. Compute overnight/intraday returns from **raw (unadjusted) open/close plus dividends added back at the ex-date open**.
- **Dividend strategies** (S024, S101) need *unadjusted* prices plus explicit dividend credits (as the catalog says), so they need a different series than every other strategy. Define two canonical series per symbol (`tr` and `raw`) and make each strategy declare which it consumes.
- **Cash-floor convention:** `BIL` started 2007; before that use the FRED 3-month bill. Excess-return strategies (S044) subtract BIL daily.

**Required change:** add a `PriceSeries` accessor in `src/framework/data_cache.py` with explicit `kind in {"tr","raw"}`, plus a unit test that SPY's 2016-2025 `tr` CAGR exceeds `raw` CAGR by 1.2-1.8 points.

## G-03 (P0) Fill conventions are optimistic or physically impossible in several places

1. **"Buy at the close of t using the close of t" (all Connors/Chan close-based systems, ~40 entries).** The shared spec acknowledges this only as "report the next-open variant". In practice an order must be sent before the closing cutoff (NYSE/Nasdaq MOC cutoffs are 15:50-15:55 ET) using a price from 15:45-15:55, not the 16:00 close. Wu & Jegadeesh (2020, cited in the Atlas section 12.5) find closing-auction price impact takes 3-5 days to dissipate, which means a *systematic* close-fill bias on reversal strategies in particular. **Add a third fill model, `moc_proxy`:** signal computed from the 15:50 bar (minute data), fill at the official close, and report all three (`close`, `moc_proxy`, `next_open`). The repo already screens at 15:55 using minute bars (README), so the machinery exists, but minute data are held only for candidate days. Until minute data are fetched for the ETF universe, `next_open` must be the primary and `close` flagged as an upper bound.
2. **Open-based signal with open-based fill** (S015, S016, S018, S022, S026, S060). Signals computed from `open(t)` cannot be filled *at* `open(t)`. Chan himself says the stocks selected from the official open differ from those selected from pre-open quotes ("signal noise"), and that the strategy "suffered from diminishing returns from 2009 onward" (l3a0 reproduction, quoting Chan 2013). Model entry at the **first-minute bar after the signal** (09:31 close) with the 15 bps RTH slippage already in `config.py`, or at the opening print with a fill-probability haircut.
3. **Gaps through stops.** The shared spec says stops are "evaluated against the session's low/high" and "assume the stop filled first". It never says what price. A stop below a gap-down open fills at the **open**, not at the stop price. Atlas section 18.6 repeats the point (Zielkowski 2026: first-bar overnight gaps break first-passage assumptions; "stops do not protect against gaps"). Rule to add: `fill = min(open, stop)` for long stops, `max(open, stop)` for short stops/buy-stops; same for targets only if open is *better* (limit orders fill at the limit or better).
4. **Stop-entry orders (S038, S039, S065):** a buy stop at the setup-bar high fills at `max(open, level)`, not at the level.
5. **Exit "at the open after the announcement" (S053, S081) includes the announcement reaction** for after-close and pre-market reporters alike. See those entries.

<a id="g-04"></a>
## G-04 (P1) Point-in-time universes are assumed but not available

The catalog repeatedly specifies "S&P 500 stocks", "SP1500", "top 500 by market cap". The repo has no historical index-membership table, and PIT market cap exists only partially (`data/enrichment/massive_mcap.parquet`, SimFin shares outstanding for 2020-11+).

**Required change:** define the research universe by a **rule that needs only prices**: at each rebalance date, rank all common stocks by trailing 60-day *median dollar volume*, take the top N (N=500 for "S&P 500-like", N=1500 for "SP1500-like"), require price >= $5 and >= 250 prior bars. This is exactly the construction the Borsa Istanbul paper uses for lack of PIT membership ("rank all eligible stocks by trailing 60-day median traded value and take the top 100"). Keep delisted names (the repo's `daily_10years` includes them) and apply a delisting-return convention (-30% for performance delistings if unknown, 0% for cash mergers) in `src/framework`. Replace every "S&P 500 stocks" / "SP1500" token in the catalog JSON with `universe: liquid_top_N` and record N.

<a id="g-05"></a>
## G-05 (P0) Multiple testing is not accounted for, and the screening thresholds are mis-calibrated

- The catalog has 105 entries, roughly 25 of them declared variants, each with "alternative fills, exits, parameters" (determinism convention: every "or" is a second run) and several with threshold ranges. Realistic trial count before any strategy is selected is **10^3**, not 105.
- With ~10 years of daily data the standard error of an annual Sharpe ratio is about 1/sqrt(10) = 0.32; the expected maximum over N=1,000 independent null trials is about 0.32 x 3.2 = **1.0**. Correlated trials lower N_eff but the Sharpe > 2 screen is not the weak link; **profit factor > 2 on <100 trades is**: at a 60-75% win rate and a 3:1 average-loser/winner asymmetry typical of Connors-style rules, PF > 2 is routinely produced by noise. Several catalog entries quote exactly this (S003: PF 2.5 on 148 trades).
- CXO's review of the Connors book, which the catalog cites for S007/S014/S030/S031/S087/S092, states that the analyses "apparently do not take data snooping bias into account", use "hypothetical backtests without transaction costs", and do not report out-of-sample tests. That is the evidential base of the whole Connors cluster.
- The Atlas (sections 10.6, 14.3, 18.8) is explicit: rule zoos of exactly this type (Rink 2023: 6,406 rules across 41 indices; Chuang et al. 2024: 38,456 rules) show in-sample outperformance with **no out-of-sample persistence**; Wiecki et al. (2016, 888 strategies) find reported backtest Sharpe ratios predict out-of-sample results poorly.

**Required changes:**
1. Make `src/backtest/partition.py::log_trials()` mandatory in every strategy runner: one registry line per *(strategy, variant, parameter set)*. `results/trial_registry.jsonl` already exists (11 lines, all atr_pivot).

3. Replace "Sharpe > 2 / PF > 2" with family-specific gates (see G-06) and a minimum of **100 independent trades (or 8 years of monthly rebalances) in dev and 30 in test**; below that, the report must print `insufficient evidence`, not a skill number.

## G-06 (P0) Define the baseline that each class of strategy must beat

The partition test's `trend` is IWM (small-cap beta) over the entry-to-exit window and `random` is random picks. Many catalog classes need a different null.

| Class | Null/baseline to add (in addition to trend/random/skill/bias) |
|---|---|
| Long-flat timing (S001-S007, S011-S013, S047, S063, S069, S089, S090) | Exposure-matched random timing: same number of trades and same time-in-market, drawn by block-bootstrapping entry days; plus buy-and-hold and 200-day SMA timing. |
| Overnight-only (S009, S017, S021, S034, S084, S105) | Unconditional close-to-open drift of the *same* instrument. A conditional overnight rule must beat the unconditional overnight premium, not just zero. |
| Cross-sectional baskets (S027-S035, S041, S049, S075, S078) | Equal-weight universe, **vol-matched with cash** (the Borsa Istanbul paper's decisive test: its 5-characteristic strategy had alpha -0.4%/yr [-6.3%, +5.5%] against it, while showing +4.4% against the cap-weighted index). |
| Rotation (S042-S044, S048, S068, S074) | Equal-weight of the same universe, SPY, 60/40; for S044 the published JPME5 figures. |
| Pairs / market-neutral (S071-S078) | Zero-beta null: hedge-ratio-shuffled pairs; random pair of same sector. |
| Event/calendar (S052-S055, S079-S086, S101) | Placebo dates: same rule applied to random non-event dates matched by day-of-week and volatility. |

Replace the blanket ">2 Sharpe / >2 PF" aspiration with class gates: e.g. rotation/factor sleeves net Sharpe >= matched baseline + 0.2 with DSR > 0.9; high-frequency swing rules net PF >= 1.3 and net expectancy >= 2x round-trip cost. The catalog's own slow sleeves publish Sharpe ratios of 0.4-0.6 (Efficiente 5: 0.58 replicated, 0.50 actual; Validea sector momentum: 11.2% return at 19% vol; Faber/Antonacci on the S&P about 11-12% CAGR), so a >2 gate would reject every slow sleeve by construction and tells you nothing about overlays.

<a id="g-07"></a>
## G-07 (P0) The partition test as implemented does not fit most catalog strategies, and its test window is too short

`docs/Notes_On_Partition_Test.md` and `src/backtest/partition.py` define total/trend/skill on a "$5,000 account, 5 positions at 20% each", with entries at 15:55, `trend_iwm` as headline trend, and `partition_split_date = 2026-06-16` with `test_months = 4` (config defaults). `evaluate._generic()` calls `simulate_account(selected, max_pos=5, ...)`.

Problems for the catalog:
1. **Test partition of ~4 months** (2026-06-16 onward) contains ~4 monthly rebalances, ~1 FOMC date, 4 turn-of-month windows, and a few dozen swing trades. Every slow/calendar strategy (28 + 15 entries) would show `skill` on noise. Use a split by *year*: dev 2016-01 to 2023-12, test 2024-01 to the last completed session, locked; for rotation strategies add combinatorial purged CV (the Atlas, section 14, recommends purged walk-forward with embargo).
2. **Adapters needed** (add to `src/framework/evaluate.py`): (a) timing strategies: trend = exposure-weighted benchmark return, random = block-bootstrapped random timing, per G-06; (b) rebalanced portfolios: random = k-of-N draws at each rebalance date; (c) market-neutral: trend = beta x market (should be ~0), (d) overnight: benchmark window = close-to-open.
3. **Sizing mismatch**: 5 x 20% of $5,000 = $1,000 per position. Decile baskets (>=50 names), 10-name baskets, 30-pair books and a 50-name market-neutral basket (S078) are not implementable at that size; fractional shares are required and MOC/OPG eligibility of fractional orders must be confirmed with the broker. Run catalog strategies at **research scale** (frictionless sizing, costs in bps) and **paper scale** ($5,000, max 5 positions) separately and state which one a number refers to.
4. AGENTS.md requires "trend, random-pick baseline, skill, bias, skill % for dev and test" in every report; the above is the minimum to make those columns meaningful.

## G-08 (P1) Cost model

- Catalog: 5 bps/side ETFs, 10-15 bps stocks. The repo's own constants are `slip_rth 0.0015` (15 bps), `slip_ext 0.004`, `backtest_commission 0.001` (10 bps/side!). These disagree with the catalog by up to 3x and with each other.
- **S021/S105 (daily SPY overnight)** pay costs 2x per day, 504 sides a year: at 5 bps/side that is 25%/yr, which exceeds any plausible overnight premium and *guarantees* failure; at ~0.5-1 bp/side (auction fills, zero commission) it is workable. The result is purely a function of the cost assumption, so run a cost-sensitivity ladder (0.5/1/2/5 bps) and report the break-even cost.
- Costs must be spread/ADV-aware for stocks (the S&P 500 and Russell-1000 constituents in dollar-volume decile 1 cost ~2-4 bps; small caps 20-60 bps). Chan's "10 bps round trip" for SP1500 is for large caps at auction prices.
- Turnover tables (annual one-way turnover x cost) must appear in every report; weekly-rebalanced decile reversal strategies (S027, S049) turn over 100%+ per week.

### S001 | RSI(2) Classic (Connors)
**Verdict:** KEEP (cluster control) | **Priority:** P2 | **Source:** OK | **Data:** ETF total-return series (G-01, G-02)

**Source check:** StockCharts ChartSchool page fetched and matches: SMA(200) filter, RSI(2) below 10 (Connors found below 5 returned more), exit above the 5-day SMA, buy before the close, no stops ("stops hurt performance").

**Findings:**
1. The page's own conclusion is that *< 5* outperformed *< 10*; the catalog baselines *< 10*. Fine for sample size, but both must be registered trials.
2. Independent evidence says the effect is regime-limited. Be The Puck (372 names, RSI(2) pullback, same rule): beat buy-and-hold Sharpe on 50/371; calls right 59% over 90,799 calls. The edge is concentrated after 2000 (G-11).
3. Close fill is the weak point (G-03). Connors "advocates the before-the-close approach" so the primary model is `moc_proxy`, with `next_open` as the alternative.
4. No-stop tails: add a registered disaster-stop variant (e.g. close < entry - 3 x ATR(14)), because the 200-SMA filter does not stop 2008/2020-style gap sequences.

**Required changes:**
- Register trials: RSI threshold {5,10}, exit {close > SMA5, RSI(2) > 70}, fill {`moc_proxy`, `next_open`, `close`}. Primary = threshold 10, SMA5 exit, `moc_proxy`.
- Run on SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA plus the 11 sector ETFs; report per-ETF and pooled.
- Compare with exposure-matched random timing (G-06) and the S006/S007 cluster; keep S001 as the cluster control.

### S015 | Chan Buy-on-Gap
**Verdict:** FIX (run as a pre-registered falsification test) | **Priority:** P2 | **Source:** OK

**Source check:** the l3a0 reproduction fetched. It confirms Chan's rules (return from previous *low* to today's open below -1 std of 90-day close-to-close returns; open above the 20-day SMA of closes; buy the 10 deepest; sell at the **same day's close**) and reproduces Chan's printed APR 8.7% / Sharpe 1.5 on **his own survivor-biased file** (497 stocks in the S&P 500 in late 2011, 2006-05 to 2012-04, **no costs**). 4. 2009+ decay in Chan's own words; our sample (2016+) is entirely post-decay.
5. Survivorship and std definition (population vs sample; Chan's two `smartstd` helpers change the printed figures: 8.7%/1.5 vs 8.4%/1.7).

**Required changes:** primary = same-day close exit (Chan); next-open = alternate; entry at the 09:31 bar close with 15 bps slippage; population std; PIT liquid-universe (G-04); record expected-count of trades (~0.5/day) in the registry; treat as a falsification test with a pre-registered expectation of ~zero skill after 2016.

### S017 | Night Trading Overnight-Bias Stock Selection
**Verdict:** KEEP | **Priority:** P1 (data already in repo) | **Source:** OK

**Source check:** SSRN 2633476 is Lachance (single author; also *Review of Financial Economics*). Abstract (via search; page itself returns 403): overnight returns are subject to highly persistent biases; a model identifies **one fifth of stocks** as having positive and statistically significant overnight biases; investing overnight in those the next year yields **twice the market's return for a third of the market beta**; these stocks average negative returns intraday; "implementation costs and issues are discussed". The catalog's quote matches. The t-stat > 2 screen is the catalog's operationalisation, not the paper's recurring-bias model (disclosed in the notes).

**Findings:**
1. **Cost drag decides the result.** A daily round trip in stocks is 2 x (half-spread + slippage) per day; at 10-15 bps per side (catalog) that is 25-38%/yr, comparable to the claimed premium. Use auction fills (MOC/MOO) with realistic imbalance slippage and report break-even cost.
2. Overnight return is measured open-to-close of different prints; **opening-print bid-ask bounce biases overnight returns upward**, especially in small names. Use the first-minute VWAP/mid for exits where minute data exist, and a liquidity floor.
3. With t > 2 about 2.3% of stocks pass by chance (one-sided), versus the paper's 20% flagged; apply Benjamini-Hochberg FDR and require persistence (formation year selects; next year evaluates; do not roll the screen daily).
4. "Top-quintile" and "t > 2" are both in the entry: pick t > 2 AND top-quintile.
5. Raw/dividend series (G-02): ex-dividend morning drops are overnight returns.

**Required changes:** implement formation (trailing 252-day mean overnight return and t-stat, FDR-controlled), hold-out evaluation next calendar year, daily overnight holds (close-to-open) with auction-fill costs, and the unconditional overnight drift of the same stocks as the null. Add Lou-Polk-Skouras (2019 JFE) style persistence of the overnight component as a robustness check (verify citation before use). Daily OHLC from `daily_10years` suffices.

**Verdict:** FIX | **Priority:** P1 (most replicated daily-bar effect) | **Source:** MISMATCH

**Required changes:** formation = 5-day return, optional 1-day skip; peer-adjust by industry (SimFin `industries.csv` or SIC) and market; liquid-top-N universe (G-04); hold 5 sessions; report raw and industry-adjusted side by side; fix the source note.

## Family 8 - Calendar Strategies (S079-S086)

<a id="s079"></a>
### S079 | Turn-of-the-Month Effect
**Verdict:** FIX (spec bug) | **Priority:** P1 | **Source:** OK

**Source check:** SSRN 917884 (McConnell & Xu, FAJ 2008) via search: "equity returns over the interval **beginning the last trading day of the month and ending three days later** are significantly higher"; all of the excess market return in 1987-2005 and 1897-2005 occurred in the four-day window; the standard deviation is not higher in the window; present in 31 of 35 countries.

**Findings:**
1. **Off-by-one.** Buying at the *close of the last trading day* and selling at the close of the third trading day of the new month earns only three daily returns (days 1-3); the four-day window starts with the last day's return, so entry must be at the close of the **second-to-last** trading day (as Be The Puck: "in at the close two trading days before month-end; out at the close on the third trading day of the new month"). `hold: 4 sessions` is inconsistent with the stated entry.
2. Erosion: Trading Garden (S&P 500, MES): 1963-today 765 trades, 58% win, CAGR 11.2% vs 10.9% buy-and-hold; since 2000 only 321 trades with CAGR 3.0% (reinvested); Be The Puck: beat buy-and-hold Sharpe on 63/372.
3. Time in market is ~19%, so Sharpe/return must be judged per unit of exposure against a random-window placebo (G-06).
4. The effect is documented on equal-weight/CRSP indexes; use SPY and IWM/RSP.

**Required changes:** fix entry to the second-to-last close; grid of windows (entry day -2..-1, exit day +2..+5) in one study (absorbs S083); placebo windows; subperiod table.

### S080 | Pre-Holiday Effect
**Verdict:** FIX | **Priority:** P3 | **Source:** PARTIAL

**Source check:** StockCharts ChartSchool fetched: the strategy is "purchase equities one or two days before a holiday... the theory is that traders lighten up their holdings (selling) before the three-day holiday... the selling pressure drives stock prices down", with results by holiday for "buy two days before / one day before, sell at year-end" and an original study of **419 closings 1928-1975**. The catalog's concept says the opposite ("prices rise on the day before holidays... the imbalance drifts prices up into the close").

**Findings:** (1) The documented anomaly (Lakonishok-Smidt 1988; Ariel 1990) is a high return *on the last trading day before the holiday*; the right test is close(t-2) -> close(t-1 before holiday) (Be The Puck's construction), not "buy 1-2 days before, sell after the holiday". (2) Holiday count is ~9-10 per year, ~90 events in 2016+ (Juneteenth since 2022); two half-days (July 3, day after Thanksgiving) behave differently. (3) Be The Puck: beat buy-and-hold Sharpe 91/372.

**Required changes:** primary = close two sessions before, exit close of the last session before the holiday; secondary = exit the first session after; exclude early-close days or report them separately; fix the concept text.

S047 only covers the Antonacci gate.
**Spec (Faber 2007, "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation"):** at each month-end, for each of 5 asset classes (SPY, EFA, IEF, GSG/DBC, IYR/VNQ) hold it (equal weight 20%) if its month-end close (total return) is above its 10-month SMA, otherwise hold BIL/short-term Treasuries; execute at the next open (primary) or month-end close. Variants: 5-asset vs 13-asset, 200-day SMA daily-checked.
**Tests:** vs S047a, S043, 60/40 and equal-weight 5 assets; 2022 episode; costs negligible (monthly).
**Data:** ETFs with dividends (G-01/02); 1928+ index data (Yahoo/FRED) for long-history validation.

<a id="s108"></a>
### S108 | Volatility-managed momentum
**Why:** Atlas 9.6/18.4/18.6: Barroso & Santa-Clara (2015) show the risk of momentum is predictable and managing it "nearly doubles the Sharpe ratio while virtually eliminating crashes"; it is the template named for volatility-scaled factor sleeves, and the Borsa paper's risk layer is the same idea. The catalog's S104 uses a drawdown gate instead.
**Spec:** the 12-1 winner portfolio (S041; long-only decile or long-short if borrow available); scale exposure each month by `w_t = sigma_target / sigma_hat_t`, with sigma_hat = realised daily-vol of the strategy's own return over the previous 6 months (126 days), annualised; sigma_target = 12% (long-short) or equal to the unscaled strategy's full-sample vol (long-only); cap at 1.0 (no leverage) and optionally at 1.5-2.0. Compare to S104's gate and to no overlay.
**Tests:** crash months (Mar-Apr 2009 analog: Apr-May 2020), turnover of the overlay, the Atlas's honest benchmark (B4: does a change-point gate beat plain vol scaling?).
**Data:** daily stock returns (D1).

<a id="s109"></a>
### S109 | Formulaic-alpha library (OHLCV subset)
**Why:** Atlas 9.1: Kakushadze (2016) publishes 101 explicit alphas as code with average holding periods of ~0.6-6.4 days, low pairwise correlation, returns tied to volatility rather than turnover; the Atlas calls it "the natural corpus and baseline". It is exactly the daily-bar, holding-1-to-6-days class this catalog targets, and it replaces ad-hoc candlestick/oscillator rules (S096-S100) with a pre-specified, correlation-aware library.
**Spec:** implement the alphas that use only open/high/low/close/volume and cross-sectional rank/delta/correlation operators (for example #101 `(close - open)/((high - low) + 0.001)`, #12 `sign(delta(volume,1)) * (-delta(close,1))`, #33 `rank(-(1 - open/close))`, #41 `sqrt(high*low) - vwap`; verify each against the paper's appendix before coding); neutralise by industry and size; each alpha traded as long-short quintiles at the close (research scale) with the paper's holding-period convention; then build the "pool" by marginal contribution (Atlas desk note: score candidates by what they add to the existing pool, not stand-alone IC; mine only on an early window then freeze).
**Tests:** DSR over all alphas tried (G-05), pairwise correlation against the catalog champions (S027, S075, S106), IC decay by horizon, capacity.
**Data:** daily OHLCV (VWAP available in the Alpaca bars used by `fetch_daily_10years.py`).

<a id="s110"></a>
### S110 | MAX/lottery exclusion overlay
**Why:** Atlas 9.4: the maximum daily return in the prior month negatively predicts returns (Bali et al., cited there as 2008; also the minimum-return variant by Aziz & Ansari 2018, concentrated in small stocks). It is cheap, price-only, and directly useful as a *filter* for the long-only baskets (S027, S029, S041, S045) which otherwise load on lottery stocks.
**Spec:** MAX(1) = highest daily return in the last 21 sessions (and MAX(5) average of top five); monthly sort; research test = long bottom decile / short top decile; practical use = exclude the top-decile MAX names from any long basket. Report effect on S027/S041 with and without the filter.
**Data:** D1.

<a id="s111"></a>
### S111 | Betting-against-beta / low-beta decile
**Why:** Atlas 9.4: Frazzini-Pedersen (2014) with the replication caveats listed there (Novy-Marx & Velikov: performance largely from non-transparent equal-weighting; Liu et al.: beta's correlation with idiosyncratic vol). Complements S035 (low-vol).
**Spec:** rolling 252-day beta vs SPY (shrunk: 0.6 x beta_ts + 0.4 x 1); monthly low-beta decile equal-weight and beta-scaled to 1; compare with S035 and the vol-matched equal-weight universe.
**Data:** D1 + SPY.

<a id="s112"></a>
### S112 | Multi-speed time-series momentum with turnover control and monitor
**Why:** Atlas 10.1: Baltas & Kosowski (2013): better volatility estimation and trend detection cut TSMOM turnover by more than a third without a significant performance penalty; Kurth et al. (2026): short-term trends in ~100 liquid futures stopped working around 2009 and depend on speed and asset class; the Atlas desk note ("trend-signal monitor") prescribes a bank of speeds with per-speed on/off flags. The catalog's S036/S037/S091 test only fast Donchian channels and without volatility targeting at the strategy level.
**Spec:** ETF universe across asset classes (SPY, EFA, EEM, TLT/IEF, LQD/HYG, GLD, GSG/DBC, USO, UUP); signals = sign of excess return over {1, 3, 6, 12} months; position = signal x (target_vol / sigma_hat) per asset, target 10%, sigma_hat = EWMA 60-day; combine speeds equally; rebalance weekly with a no-trade band; per-speed rolling 36-month net-Sharpe monitor that scales a speed's weight to zero when below threshold.
**Tests:** vs S036/S037, vs buy-and-hold risk parity; subperiods (2016-19, 2020, 2022); turnover.
**Data:** ETFs (G-01) including USO/UUP (UUP not present).

<a id="s113"></a>
### S113 | Cluster-then-test pairs with structural-break stop
**Why:** Atlas 9.7: cluster-then-test pair selection (Mantegna correlation trees; Millington & Sanchez Granero; Korniejczuk & Lepaczuk graph clustering + Kelly), break filters (Dai et al. 2025 VECM-based), and a sober out-of-sample check (Wilkens 2025: does pairs trading still work in Germany 2000-2023). The catalog's S071 uses a hand-listed ETF pair set and monthly ADF on one window.
**Spec:** each month, formation window 252 days; cluster stocks within sector by correlation distance (hierarchical, average linkage); in each cluster test Engle-Granger on log prices with an estimated hedge ratio (p < 0.05 and half-life 5-30 days, Hurst < 0.5); trade z-score entry 2.0, exit 0.5, stop |z| > 4 or when a rolling ADF/break test fails; max hold 3 x half-life; dollar-neutral; costs both legs; selection window and trading window strictly disjoint.
**Tests:** false-positive count vs 5% nominal; shuffled-sector null; borrow scenarios (G-09); research scale only.
**Data:** D1 + sector map; shorting.

<a id="s114"></a>
### S114 | Closing-auction price-pressure reversal
**Why:** Atlas 12.5: Wu & Jegadeesh (2020) report that closing-auction temporary price impact "takes three to five days to dissipate and that strategies exploiting the impact and reversals are profitable"; this is the microstructure source of S027/S001-type reversals and a direct test of the close-fill convention (G-03.1).
**Spec:** for each stock/day compute `auction_move = close / price at 15:50 - 1` (minute bars, mid or last trade) and the closing-auction volume share if available; buy the most negative decile (research) next to the close, hold 3-5 sessions; sell the most positive decile as a hedge leg (optional).
**Data:** 1-minute bars for the whole liquid universe (the repo has minute bars only for candidate days; this needs a broader fetch - `scripts/fetch_minute_universe.py`). Until then, run a daily proxy (IBS/close-location + volume shock).

<a id="s115"></a>
### S115 | CVR3 VIX market timing
**Why:** StockCharts ChartSchool documents Connors & Landry's CVR3 as a Connors-lineage VIX rule, adjacent to S061 but with explicit, codeable conditions.
**Spec (from the page):** **Buy signal** (all on the VIX bar): (1) daily low above the 10-day MA; (2) daily close at least 10% above its 10-day MA (PPO(1,10,1) >= 10 on the EMA form); (3) close below open (black candle). **Sell signal:** (1) high below the 10-day MA; (2) close at least 10% below it (PPO <= -10); (3) close above open. The page suggests rule windows (all three within 3 days) to raise signal frequency and Connors/Landry suggested tight stops. Trade SPY at the next close/open; exit on the sell signal or a time stop.
**Tests:** event table (few signals per year), compare with S061, G-05 trial accounting.
**Data:** VIX OHLC (G-01).

### S116 | Halloween / sell-in-May seasonal
**Why:** the calendar family has no seasonal-window rule; Be The Puck's "Halloween Indicator" beat buy-and-hold Sharpe on **121/372** (best of the calendar rules there) and Trading Garden's "Sell in May" ranks third in the 1963-today table (CAGR 11.2%, 75% win, 64 trades, drawdown 33.8%).

<a id="s117"></a>
### S117 | Announcement-return (EAR) drift - consensus-free PEAD
**Why:** S054 cannot be run without consensus estimates and has a look-ahead as written. A standard consensus-free construction uses the announcement-window abnormal return (Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok 1996 *(verify)*) and SUE from reported EPS (seasonal random walk), both buildable from SimFin (`Publish Date`, quarterly EPS inputs) plus prices.
**Spec:** event date = SimFin `Publish Date` (or EDGAR 8-K 2.02 from 2023-09 for BMO/AMC timing); EAR = cumulative return from t-1 to t+1 around the release minus SPY; SUE = (EPS_q - EPS_{q-4}) / std of the last 8 such differences; rank against **trailing** distributions (previous four quarters), no same-season look-ahead; buy top-decile EAR and top-decile SUE (combination = both) at the open of t+2 (after the reaction), hold 10/21/42 sessions; short bottom decile separately.
**Tests:** long-short vs matched-size/industry controls, post-2016 decay (Martineau *(verify)*), ESS by calendar season.
**Data:** SimFin quarterly + daily prices (D1).

<a id="s118"></a>
### S118 | Live-edge LLM nowcast forward test
**Why:** S056/S057/S058/S060 cannot be backtested cleanly. Atlas 15.2 describes Chen & Pu (2026), who run an LLM daily on Russell 1000 stocks from April 2025 so predictions are collected at the live edge and are "free of look-ahead by construction" (finding: the model identifies top winners but not the rest of the ranking, i.e. a long-only tilt candidate); Atlas 15.6 lists leakage audits (masked identifiers, cross-model disagreement, post-cutoff windows).
**Spec:** each day at 15:30 ET, send (headline, summary, ticker) batches for the day's news to a frozen model; store raw outputs with vendor and ingestion timestamps in SQLite; score = polarity x topic weight (S058 topics); paper-trade the top-decile long-only at the close with next-open exits for >= 6 months before any capital; track IC decay (Lopez-Lira & Tang: returns decline with LLM adoption) and cost.
**Data:** live news; existing Alpaca news client (`src/data/news_client.py`).

<a id="s119"></a>
### S119 | Index-inclusion / reconstitution liquidity-provision events
**Why:** Atlas 12.8 (Bogousslavsky & Muravyev 2023): closing auctions carry a growing share of volume, auction volume jumps on index additions; events are scheduled and price pressure from forced flows is a documented liquidity-provision premium (Atlas 12.5 bridge: "price pressure... what the factors literature files as a liquidity-provision premium").
**Spec (hypothesis):** around S&P index additions/deletions and the annual Russell reconstitution (late June), fade the announcement-to-effective-date move (buy deletions at the effective-date close pressure, sell/avoid additions) and measure 5-day reversal.
**Data:** index-change announcements (not in repo; S&P press releases, FTSE Russell lists): blocked until sourced.

<a id="s120"></a>
### S120 | Moving-average timing on volatility-sorted portfolios
**Why:** Atlas 10.1: Han et al. (2013) apply a moving-average timing rule to portfolios sorted by volatility and report abnormal returns versus CAPM and FF3, strongest for high-volatility portfolios; "the same rule behaves differently depending on the risk of the instrument". It reframes S089/S090 as a conditioning study.
**Spec (verify parameters in the paper):** monthly vol-decile portfolios (60-day vol); for each decile hold the portfolio when its price index is above its 10-day or 20-day MA, else cash/SPY; compare with the always-invested portfolio by decile.
**Data:** D1.
